# Title, Abstract, and Keywords Standard

Apply this standard to every paper. Score the abstract as an independent 10-point category. Review the title and keywords alongside it, but do not deduct the same defect again under formatting.

## 1. Title

Require a concise, accurate and representative title that:

- identifies the main research object or core problem;
- reflects a key model or research method when this improves specificity;
- agrees with the work actually performed.

Flag titles that are excessively long, use vague forms such as `关于……的研究`, claim a model absent from the paper, or fail to distinguish the work. Treat title quality as contextual evidence for front-matter quality; do not reduce the 10-point abstract score by more than 0.5 solely for a weak title.

## 2. Abstract structure

Use continuous prose paragraphs. For a five-question data-based problem, expect this default structure:

1. **Opening paragraph:** exactly two sentences where practical. Sentence one gives the real-world background/significance; sentence two states the core tasks and overall objective.
2. **Data-processing paragraph:** state only operations actually performed, such as reading, cleaning, missing/anomaly treatment, transformations, indicator construction or feature extraction. Omit this paragraph when the work has no substantive data-processing stage.
3. **One paragraph per actual problem:** for each question, state in order (a) the task, (b) model/algorithm and principal solution process, and (c) key result—representative values, laws, ranking, prediction, optimum or evaluation conclusion.
4. **Closing paragraph:** connect the questions, summarize effectiveness, application value and main conclusions. Mention genuine model combinations, algorithm improvements, indicator innovation, parameter optimization or robustness only when present.

Adapt paragraph count to the actual problem. Fewer than five questions means fewer problem paragraphs. Keep multiple subquestions from the same numbered question in one coherent paragraph. Do not invent extra question numbers. The commonly described “eight paragraphs” is therefore a five-question template, not a universal requirement.

## 3. Abstract content rules

Require formal, objective and concise academic language. The abstract must stand alone and make clear:

- what concrete problem is studied;
- what research/modeling work is performed;
- what principal model or algorithm is used;
- what main result is obtained.

Prefer representative numerical results when the paper contains them. Never invent a value absent from the paper or computations; use a qualitative conclusion or an explicit result placeholder only when drafting before calculation. Do not include formulas, code, figure/table numbers or references. Do not explain derivations in detail. Avoid mechanical `本文首先、然后、最后` narration, repetition and unnecessary model lists.

## 4. Ten-point scoring anchors

Apply the universal judge ceiling after holistic assessment: the abstract can earn at most 9.0/10. A nominal 9-10 quality abstract therefore receives 9.0, with the difference labeled `评委满分保留（该项90%封顶）` rather than a writing defect.

Use holistic judgment with the following anchors:

- **0:** absent.
- **1-2:** fragment, placeholder, essentially unreadable, materially fabricated, or seriously contradicts the paper; not a completed abstract.
- **3-4:** a completed abstract receives a starting score of 3 when it has no exceptionally serious defect. It identifies some task or method but omits most results, is process-heavy, vague, poorly organized, or hard for a non-modeler to follow.
- **5-6:** covers most tasks and methods, but task-method-result chains are incomplete; results are vague, important questions are omitted, or excessive jargon prevents independent understanding.
- **7-8:** passes the lay-reader test: a person unfamiliar with mathematical modeling can understand the concrete problem, research performed, models used and results obtained. It covers the actual questions with substantially complete task-method-result logic. Give 8 when results are specific and coverage/clarity are strong, with only minor omissions.
- **9-10:** fully and efficiently represents the paper: accurate two-sentence opening where appropriate, truthful data processing, complete per-question task-model-solution-result summaries, representative quantitative results, coherent closing synthesis, and perfect consistency with the body. Give 10 only when it is exceptionally concise, informative and self-contained with no material omission.

The 3-point floor applies only to a genuinely completed abstract without a major integrity or consistency defect. A few sentences, boilerplate, invented results, or an abstract unrelated to the paper do not qualify.

## 5. Lay-reader test

Before assigning 7 or above, answer all four questions using only the abstract:

1. What real problem is being solved?
2. What did the team actually analyze or construct?
3. What principal models/algorithms were used for each task?
4. What concrete results or conclusions were obtained?

If any answer is materially unavailable, normally cap the abstract at 6. If the information exists but cannot be understood without reading the body, normally cap at 6.

## 6. Keywords

Expect 4-5 keywords separated by semicolons. Together they should cover:

1. the research object or core problem;
2. principal mathematical model(s);
3. key solution algorithm(s) or technical method(s).

Use professional terms that actually occur in the paper. Reject generic terms such as `数学建模`, `数据分析`, or `结果研究`, and any model/algorithm not used. Treat keyword quality as contextual evidence; do not reduce the abstract score by more than 0.5 solely for keyword defects and do not double-count the same issue under formatting.
